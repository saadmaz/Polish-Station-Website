BODY:

Wax and sealants wear off in weeks. Both ceramic and graphene coatings chemically bond to the clear coat instead of sitting on top of it, forming a hard, hydrophobic layer that protects against UV fading, bird droppings, road salt, and light abrasion for years. The question isn't really "which one works" - both do. It's which one fits your car and how you use it.

## What They Have in Common

- Both require full paint correction beforehand - a coating is transparent and permanent, so it locks in whatever condition the paint is in when it's applied.
- Both create a hard, glass-like shield that makes a car noticeably easier to keep clean.
- Both produce the hydrophobic "sheeting" effect where water beads and rolls off instead of pooling.
- Both need a proper curing period, with no water or contact during the first stretch (see our [aftercare guide](/blog/ceramic-coating-aftercare) for the full timeline).

## Where They Differ

### Heat Dissipation

Graphene is a better thermal conductor than pure ceramic, so a graphene coating spreads and releases heat from the surface more efficiently. This matters most on dark-colored paint, which absorbs significantly more heat in direct sun than lighter colors - a graphene coating helps that heat move on rather than building up at the surface.

### Flexibility

Graphene coatings have slightly more flexibility than ceramic. That's a small detail on solid factory paint, but it matters more on vehicle wraps and paint protection film (PPF), which flex and shift slightly more than hard paint - a more rigid coating is more prone to micro-cracking on those surfaces over time.

### Durability

At Polish Station:

- **10H Ceramic Coating** - our toughest ceramic shield, also 3-year durability, for maximum hardness.
- **Graphene Coating** - 5-year durability, our longest-lasting protective option.

The "H" rating (like 9H or 10H) refers to pencil hardness test results for scratch resistance, not durability - a 10H coating is harder against light scratching, while graphene's longer-rated lifespan comes from a different part of its chemistry.

### Gloss and Finish

Both deliver a high-gloss finish. Graphene has a reputation for a very slightly deeper, warmer gloss on darker colors, though the difference is subtle enough that most owners choose based on durability and heat performance rather than looks alone.

## So Which One Should You Get?

- **Choose ceramic (9H or 10H)** if you want proven, long-standing coating chemistry with strong hardness and 3-year durability, especially on lighter-colored paint.
- **Choose graphene** if your car is dark-colored and sits in direct sun often, if it has a wrap or PPF, or if you want the longest durability window we offer.
