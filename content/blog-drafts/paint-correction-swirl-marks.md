BODY:

Stand behind a dark-colored car on a sunny day and you'll usually see it: a haze of fine, circular scratches swirling across every panel, especially visible at an angle to the light. These are swirl marks, and almost every daily-driven car has them, whether the owner has noticed yet or not.

## What Swirl Marks Actually Are

Swirl marks are thousands of microscopic scratches in the clear coat - the transparent top layer that sits over a car's color coat and gives it depth and gloss. They're not on the surface of the paint the way dust is; they're physically etched into it. That's why washing, waxing, or a quick detail spray can make them look temporarily better (by filling them with oils) but never actually removes them.

## What Causes Them

Swirl marks build up gradually from normal contact with the paint:

- **Automatic car washes** - the brushes and cloth strips pick up grit from every car that passes through and drag it across yours.
- **Washing with the wrong tools** - dirty sponges, reused towels, or washing without the two-bucket method grinds trapped dirt into the paint instead of lifting it away.
- **Drying with a dry towel** - wiping a wet car with anything less than a clean, dedicated microfiber towel is one of the most common causes.
- **Dust and pollen settling and being wiped off** - even a "quick wipe" with the wrong cloth adds up over months.

None of this is a sign of neglect - it's the ordinary cost of driving and washing a car regularly. It's also why a barely-visible layer of swirls under fluorescent light can look dramatically worse the moment direct sunlight hits the panel.

## Why Washing More Carefully Doesn't Fix Existing Swirls

Once a scratch is in the clear coat, no amount of gentle washing removes it - washing only prevents new ones from forming as quickly. Fixing existing swirl marks requires physically levelling the clear coat around the scratch until it's gone, which is what paint correction does.

## How Paint Correction Works

At Polish Station, paint correction runs through the same core stages regardless of how heavy the swirling is:

1. **Pre-wash** - an active foam pass dissolves dirt and road grime before anything touches the paint, so the correction step itself doesn't introduce new scratches.
2. **Paint correction** - a machine polisher, loaded with cutting or finishing compound depending on paint hardness and defect depth, works in stages to safely remove swirl marks, scratches, and haze.
3. **Surface preparation** - a specialized cleaning agent strips away any leftover polish oils and waxes, since those would otherwise block a coating from bonding properly afterward.
4. **Finish inspection** - the panel is checked under raking light (light held at a low angle) which reveals defects that are invisible under normal lighting, to confirm nothing was missed.

For paint that's only lightly faded rather than heavily swirled, a lighter single-stage [Cut & Polish](/services/cut-and-polish) service brings back gloss and depth without the multi-stage time investment full correction requires.

This sequencing isn't optional if a [ceramic](/services/ceramic-coating) or [graphene coating](/services/graphene-coating) is going on afterward: a coating is transparent and permanent once cured, so whatever swirls and scratches are still in the paint when it's applied get sealed in for years.
